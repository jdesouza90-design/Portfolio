Sign-in with Ethereum

Sign-in with Ethereum lets people log in with a wallet instead of a username and password. Their identity data stays under their control, not a provider’s.

Role
Product Design Manager
Team
Design, engineering and identity product
Validation
2 rounds of user testing
Shipped
December 2021

My role

Design direction

I led design, engineering and identity product through the core tension: wallet-based identity with no friction for people who’d never used a wallet. The consumer and developer-facing teams shipped one consistent experience.

Validation and learning

Two rounds of user testing ran alongside delivery. I synthesized the findings across designers and fed them back into the roadmap.

User advocacy

I set the principles for how we explained self-custodied identity to mainstream users, and reviewed every design decision against them before handoff.

Results

Wallet login was a pattern built for people already in web3. We kept the shape of an ordinary login page instead: one button, a wallet prompt, a signed message. There are no published usage metrics, so the outcome is qualitative.

The problem

The existing pattern spoke in seed phrases and signatures. For anyone outside web3, it read as a different product, not a login.

  • AutonomyPeople should hold their own identity data and decide how it’s used.
  • SafetyThe key never leaves the wallet: the user signs a message and the server only verifies the signature.
  • AdoptionIt had to look and behave like the login people already know.

The design

The flow keeps the familiar shape of a login page. No seed phrases, no new vocabulary, and the profile metadata developers need arrives with the session.
