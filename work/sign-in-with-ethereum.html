Auth0 · Identity · Web3 login

Sign-in with Ethereum

Sign-in with Ethereum lets people log in with an Ethereum wallet instead of a username and password, so their identity data stays under their control, not a provider's.

My roleProduct Design Manager. Led a cross-functional team through two rounds of user testing and delivery.
TeamDesign, engineering and identity product, across Auth0's consumer and developer-facing teams.
Validation2 rounds of user testing, fed back into roadmap decisions.
TypeWallet-based authentication for people new to web3.

Summary

Wallet-based login was, at the time, a pattern built for people already familiar with web3. We kept the shape of an ordinary login page instead: one button, a wallet prompt, a signed message. No seed phrases, no new vocabulary, and the profile metadata developers need arrives with the session. The design was validated over two rounds of user testing before shipping across Auth0's consumer and developer-facing surfaces. There are no published usage metrics for this project, so the outcome below is qualitative.

The problem

Wallet login assumed you already knew web3

  • AutonomyPeople should hold their own identity data and decide how it's used.
  • SafetyThe key never leaves the wallet: the user signs a message and the server only verifies the signature.
  • AdoptionIt had to look and behave like the login people already know.

My role

Direction, validation, advocacy

Design direction

I led design, engineering and identity product through the core tension: wallet-based identity with no friction for people who'd never used a wallet. The consumer and developer-facing teams shipped one consistent experience.

Validation and learning

Two rounds of user testing ran alongside delivery. I synthesized the findings across designers and fed them back into the roadmap.

User advocacy

I set the principles for how we explained self-custodied identity to mainstream users, and reviewed every design decision against them before handoff.

The design

A wallet login that reads like any other login

The flow keeps the familiar shape of a login page. One button, a wallet prompt and a signed message: no seed phrases, no new vocabulary, and the profile metadata developers need arrives with the session.
