# Voice and tone

How everything on john-desouza.com is written. Read this before editing any copy.

## Who is reading

Hiring managers in product and design, skimming on a phone between meetings. Most will read the headings, the numbers and one paragraph. Write for the skim. Reward anyone who reads further.

## The voice

A design director explaining the work to a peer across a table. Plain words, specific claims, nothing to prove. Confident because the results are, not because the adjectives are. Candid about what went wrong: the retro sections and the Ledger complaint are the most credible things on the site.

Not a deck. Not a resume. Not a brand.

The tone barely moves. It is warmest in Contact and driest in Results, and both are still the same person.

## Five rules

**1. Outcome first.** The heading carries the point. The first sentence carries the specifics. A reader who stops after either one has the gist.
> Turned a loan decline into a second offer instead of a dead end.

**2. Short.** Headings under eight words. Paragraphs under three sentences. Sentences under twenty words. A sentence that lists four things separated by commas is a resume bullet. Cut it.

**3. Concrete.** Name the thing: the decline page, the progress bar, the pool gauge. Use the number when there is one: 3 hours, 25M LINK, $7.56M. An adjective usually means the number is missing. "Intuitive", "seamless", "transparent" and "real-time" say nothing. Say what it does instead.

**4. Defensible.** Say "sized" or "opportunity" when a result is not realized. Say "no published metrics" plainly. Never "zero-risk". Quotes are verbatim, rough grammar included, and always say who said them.

**5. A person, not a resume.** "I" for decisions and opinions. "We" for what the team shipped. Every sentence has a subject. No fragments that open with a verb ("Coordinated design, product and engineering...") and no stacked verbs (coordinated, managed, facilitated, drove, ensured).

## Shape of a section

Every section on the site has the same three parts, in this order.

| Part | Job | Rules |
|---|---|---|
| Eyebrow | Says what kind of section this is | One to three words: The problem, My role, Results, What I'd do differently |
| Heading | Says the point of the section | Sentence case, under eight words. Never repeats a number shown right below it. Home page headings are statements and end in a full stop; case-study section headings are fragments and don't |
| Body | Gives the specifics | One to three sentences, then stop |

Cards and triads: a one- or two-word label, then one full sentence with a subject. Not a gerund fragment ("Minimizing cognitive load at key decision points").

## Words

| Use | Instead of |
|---|---|
| the decline, the upload, the offer, the pool | the experience, the touchpoint, the solution |
| shipped, launched, replaced, cut, moved, sized, tested | delivered, drove, facilitated, ensured, leveraged, empowered |
| product, engineering and compliance (name them) | stakeholders, cross-functional partners |
| applicants, developers, people staking LINK | end users |
| a hard moment, a confusing step | a high-stakes emotional moment, cognitive load |
| what the design did | seamless, intuitive, robust, game-changing, best-in-class |

## Mechanics

- **Sentence case** for everything: headings, eyebrows, buttons, labels, tags.
- **Numbers.** In prose, spell out one to nine and use numerals from 10 up: two designers, 13 years, 25M LINK, $7.56M. Spell out any number that opens a sentence. In meta lines, stats, charts and tables, always numerals: 2 designers · 5 months.
- **Lists** have no serial comma: product, engineering and compliance.
- **Punctuation** is full stops, commas and colons. No em dashes, semicolons or exclamation marks outside a quote. The one exception is the page-title separator (Verifications — John DeSouza). Arrows belong in diagrams and the metric chain, not in prose. 0→1 is a term, not an arrow, and is fine anywhere.
- **Abbreviations** are spelled out on first use on each page. Vehicle Equity and Home Secured, not VEL and HSL. General manager, not GM. Tickers (LINK) and A/B are fine.
- **Names** as the company writes them: Best Egg, Chainlink Labs, Auth0, Sign-in with Ethereum, Staking v0.1.
- **Quotes** keep the speaker's grammar. Attribution is a label plus a source (Product confusion · Applicant interview). Public posts get the handle and the date.

## The test

Read it out loud. If you would not say it to a peer across a table, rewrite it.
