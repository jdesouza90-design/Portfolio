How I lead

I set strategy clearly enough that the team can make its own calls, then spend my time on feedback and quality rather than approvals. I sit with product leadership so the outcomes we design against are ones design helped define.

Coaching and autonomy

Strategy sets the boundaries

I define the design strategy and the framework decisions get made against. Inside it, product designers decide without waiting on me. A team that has to route every call through its leader stops scaling at one person.

Feedback is the job

Most of my week goes into feedback: weekly reviews, structured critique and time on specific work with specific people. Consistency matters more than volume, and it’s how strategy stays real.

I hold the quality bar

I review against the standard rather than my own taste, which means the bar is something the team can apply themselves: spacing and layout, design system usage, color and contrast, accessibility.

A seat in product leadership

I sit with product leadership to help define what the products are trying to achieve, so design is shaping the outcome rather than reacting to a roadmap it had no part in setting.

AI in the process

My teams build agents for the slow, repetitive parts of the work. One audits our Figma library every month and writes the exact fix for each issue it finds. Another turns a flow into a prototype customers can try while it’s still cheap to change.

Every agent works from a brief and a set of rules, and someone on the team reviews what it produces. The agents make us faster. The review keeps the quality up.

I’m deliberate about what we adopt. A new tool has to fit a real need before the team takes it on, not just be the newest thing. It keeps its place only while it earns it.

How I track design

Design is accountable at three levels at once, and they feed each other. Craft moves behavior, behavior moves the number the business already reports. Any surface I own carries all three, so a decision can be traced end to end.

Spacing and layout QA, design system component usage, color and contrast, accessibility violations. The diagnostics that keep quality from drifting once a team is moving fast.

It shows up as a design decision

Completion rate, time to decision, drop-off at each step. At least one has to be perceptual, like effort or confidence, or the layer only measures speed.

It shows up as a behavior change

Conversion, activation, retention. Design answers to the same number the business already reports, not a parallel one invented for design.

It shows up as a business result
